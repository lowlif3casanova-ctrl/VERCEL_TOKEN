# Research dossier

This dossier is the raw material behind the session design. Six research agents each took one lens, searched the literature and the traditions, and returned findings with an evidence level. A separate skeptic agent then fact-checked every lens; its corrections are listed after each lens and take precedence over the original finding wherever they conflict. Evidence levels: *strong* / *moderate* / *weak* / *anecdotal* are about scientific support; *traditional* means a lineage's own teaching, reported as such; *none* means a modern claim with no support.

## 1. The science of binaural beats and auditory entrainment

*Lens scope:* THE SCIENCE OF BINAURAL BEATS AND AUDITORY ENTRAINMENT (psychoacoustic limits, EEG entrainment evidence, meta-analyses, drumming/auditory driving, 40 Hz gamma, pineal and Schumann claims, safety)

### Psychoacoustic limits of binaural beats (Licklider, Webster & Hedlun)

- **Evidence level:** **strong**
- **Finding:** The classic JASA frequency-limit study is Licklider, Webster & Hedlun 1950 (not 1957), JASA 22:468-473. The largest beat frequency that is still heard is about 35 Hz, and it occurs for carriers of roughly 350-500 Hz; beats can be heard with carriers above 1000 Hz only when the frequency difference is very small and the listener attends carefully, so the upper carrier limit depends strongly on the beat frequency.
- **Usable parameters:** Carrier 350-500 Hz for the strongest beat percept; keep binaural beat frequency <= 30-35 Hz; carriers above ~1000 Hz only work for beats of a few Hz; a later psychoacoustic study put the upper carrier limit at ~1214 Hz for a 6 Hz beat and found sensitivity for 1000-1300 Hz carriers only at ~10 Hz beats.
- **Notes:** The task text's '1957' is a citation error that propagates through wellness sites; cite 1950.
- **Source:** Licklider JCR, Webster JC, Hedlun JM (1950) On the frequency limits of binaural beats. J Acoust Soc Am 22:468-473. https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8398ecc34c2bba8301f2df176cf15df6cf90d8ed ; Grose, Buss & Hall 2012 Hear Res 285:40-45 (PMC3299837) summarising it

### Oster 1973 Scientific American

- **Evidence level:** **strong**
- **Finding:** Oster's 'Auditory Beats in the Brain' (Sci Am 229(4):94-102, Oct 1973, PMID 4727697) reported that binaural beats are perceived best with carriers near 440 Hz, that at 440 Hz beats up to roughly 26 Hz (max ~30 Hz) are audible, that the beat percept fades as the carrier rises and disappears above about 1000 Hz, and that the beat keeps roughly the same apparent strength regardless of the relative intensities of the two tones. He proposed beats as a diagnostic tool (some Parkinson's patients could not hear them; women's sensitivity varied with the menstrual cycle). He did not claim brainwave entrainment.
- **Usable parameters:** Carrier ~440 Hz (range ~300-500 Hz); binaural beat <= 26-30 Hz; interaural level mismatch of several dB is tolerated. The 'beats heard even when one tone is sub-threshold' claim (Groen 1964) was NOT replicated by Gu et al. 1995 ('Failure to hear binaural beats below threshold'), so keep both ears at clearly audible, roughly equal level.
- **Notes:** Oster's paper is the origin of almost every popular binaural-beat claim, but the 'entrainment' extrapolation came later (Monroe Institute).
- **Source:** Oster G (1973) Auditory beats in the brain. Scientific American 229(4):94-102. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4727697/ ; summary https://www.binauralbeatsmeditation.com/dr-gerald-oster-auditory-beats-in-the-brain/

### Perrott & Nelson 1969 beat-detection limits

- **Evidence level:** **strong**
- **Finding:** Perrott & Nelson (JASA 46(6):1477-1481, doi 10.1121/1.1911890) found that beat-rate detection is best between 10 and 15 Hz, declines at higher rates and reaches the 50% detection level at about 30 Hz; carriers above ~1500 Hz are used in later work specifically because they do NOT produce a binaural beat percept. The upper limit depends on the frequency difference, the psychophysical procedure and the threshold criterion.
- **Usable parameters:** Binaural beats are most salient at 10-15 Hz; 30 Hz is the practical ceiling (50% detection); never use carriers above ~1500 Hz for a binaural beat.
- **Source:** Perrott DR, Nelson MA (1969) Limits for the detection of binaural beats. J Acoust Soc Am 46:1477-1481; cited in Grose, Buss & Hall 2012 (https://cdr.lib.unc.edu/downloads/x059cf72h) and Ingendoh et al. 2023 (https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0286023)

### Binaural-beat salience and ASSR (Schwarz & Taylor 2005; Grose 2012)

- **Evidence level:** **strong**
- **Finding:** Schwarz & Taylor (Clin Neurophysiol 2005;116:658-668, PMID 15721080) recorded a 40 Hz auditory steady-state response to binaural beats only at a low mean tone frequency (400 Hz); it was undetectable above 3 kHz and weaker than the acoustic (monaural) beat response, which was present at both low and high carriers. Grose, Buss & Hall 2012 showed binaural-beat salience at a 500 Hz carrier (rates 4, 8, 16, 32 Hz) equals only a shallow sinusoidal amplitude modulation, i.e. the percept is weak though all 12 listeners heard it; Grose & Mamo 2012 found no response at 810/850 Hz in most listeners.
- **Usable parameters:** Carrier ~400-500 Hz for binaural beats; for a strong 40 Hz response use monaural beats, amplitude-modulated tones or click trains instead of binaural beats; expect binaural beats to be a subtle percept and do not bury them under loud music.
- **Source:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15721080/ ; Grose JH, Buss E, Hall JW (2012) Binaural beat salience. Hear Res 285:40-45 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3299837/

### Meta-analysis: Garcia-Argibay, Santed & Reales 2019

- **Evidence level:** moderate
- **Finding:** Across 22 studies / 35 effect sizes (memory, attention, anxiety, pain) binaural beats produced a medium, significant overall effect g = 0.45. Time under exposure was a significant positive moderator (secondary summaries give '>9 min' or '8-9 min minimum'); exposure before the task, or before and during, outperformed exposure only during the task; the effect's size and direction depended on beat frequency; adding white or pink noise did not appear necessary; effects were stronger for beats alone than beats embedded in music.
- **Usable parameters:** Minimum ~10 min of continuous beat exposure per intended state (longer is better); start the beat before any task/meditation and keep it running; pink noise is optional, not required; avoid embedding the beat inside dense music; choose beat frequency by goal (theta/alpha for anxiety, beta/gamma for attention/memory).
- **Notes:** The '9 minutes' cut-off comes from Basu & Banerjee's reading of this paper, not from the abstract itself.
- **Source:** Garcia-Argibay M, Santed MA, Reales JM (2019) Psychological Research 83(2):357-372, doi 10.1007/s00426-018-1066-8 (online Aug 2018). https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326784313 ; Europe PMC abstract

### Meta-analysis: Basu & Banerjee 2023 (memory and attention)

- **Evidence level:** moderate
- **Finding:** 15 studies / 31 effect sizes gave an overall medium effect g = 0.40 for binaural beats on memory and attention, with conflicting findings about which band (theta vs beta) helps which task; the authors call for more rigorous designs.
- **Usable parameters:** Expect small-to-medium behavioural effects at best; beta (14-24 Hz) and gamma (40 Hz) beats are the ones most often linked to attention/memory gains; theta beats have sometimes impaired recall.
- **Source:** Basu S, Banerjee B (2023) Psychological Research 87:951-963, doi 10.1007/s00426-022-01706-7. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00426-022-01706-7

### Systematic review of EEG entrainment: Ingendoh, Posny & Heine 2023

- **Evidence level:** **strong**
- **Finding:** Of 14 published EEG studies of binaural-beat stimulation, 5 reported results consistent with the brainwave-entrainment hypothesis, 8 contradicted it and 1 was mixed; methods were too heterogeneous (carrier, beat, duration, noise, EEG analysis) to compare, and the authors conclude the entrainment literature is 'inconclusive at best'.
- **Usable parameters:** Any guide must say that frequency-following 'entrainment' of the whole cortex by binaural beats is NOT established; the reliable findings are the weak steady-state response at the beat frequency plus modest mood/anxiety effects.
- **Notes:** This is the single most important honesty anchor for the guide.
- **Source:** Ingendoh RM, Posny ES, Heine A (2023) PLoS ONE 18(5):e0286023, doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0286023. https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0286023

### Review: Chaieb, Wilpert, Reber & Fell 2015

- **Evidence level:** moderate
- **Finding:** The Frontiers in Psychiatry review finds most proposed cognitive effects isolated or contradictory; the most consistent effect is reduced anxiety. Parameters that matter: stimulus duration, carrier frequency and background noise (white/pink noise may strengthen the beat percept); older listeners detect gamma-range binaural beats less accurately; sex and menstrual-cycle effects are reported; attention to the stimulus matters. Reviewed studies used carriers of 250, 400 and 1000 Hz and beats of 1.5-4 Hz, 5, 7, 10, 16, 24 and 40 Hz.
- **Usable parameters:** Carriers 250-400 Hz; a light pink-noise bed is acceptable and may help the percept; for anxiolysis use theta-alpha beats (5-10 Hz); for gamma use lower carriers and monaural/isochronic delivery if the listener is older.
- **Source:** Chaieb L, Wilpert EC, Reber TP, Fell J (2015) Front Psychiatry 6:70, doi 10.3389/fpsyt.2015.00070. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4428073/

### Binaural vs monaural entrainment strength: Orozco Perez, Dumas & Lehmann 2020

- **Evidence level:** **strong**
- **Finding:** In 16 adults (70 dB SPL per ear; 8-min baseline then four 8-min blocks) with theta (7 Hz: 396.5/403.5 Hz) and gamma (40 Hz: 380/420 Hz) beats, both binaural and monaural beats produced a steady-state response at the beat frequency, but monaural beats were stronger at both 7 and 40 Hz (monaural gamma peaked highest). Only binaural beats produced cross-frequency effects (binaural theta drove 40 Hz activity; binaural gamma drove a widespread alpha connectivity pattern). Neither changed self-reported relaxation or absorption.
- **Usable parameters:** Carrier ~400 Hz; 70 dB SPL; 8-min blocks are enough to measure an SSR; use monaural (same-ear mixed) beats or isochronic pulses for the strongest cortical following, and binaural beats where cross-frequency / network effects are the goal.
- **Notes:** Press summary: 'binaural beats synchronize brain activity, don't affect mood' (ScienceDaily 2020-02-17).
- **Source:** Orozco Perez HD, Dumas G, Lehmann A (2020) eNeuro 7(2):ENEURO.0232-19.2020. https://www.eneuro.org/content/7/2/ENEURO.0232-19.2020

### 6 Hz theta binaural beat: Jirakittayakorn & Wongsawat 2017

- **Evidence level:** moderate
- **Finding:** 28 participants (17 active, 11 control) heard a 6 Hz binaural beat (250 Hz left / 256 Hz right, 65 dB SPL) for 30 min. Absolute theta power rose at nearly all sites (except T4) within 10 min, including frontal-midline theta at Fz; 10 min was named the most effective duration; theta did not stay elevated after the sound stopped. Mood (BRUMS): 'worried' fell, but anger, depression, fatigue and confusion items rose and vigor fell, which the authors attribute to possible over-stimulation.
- **Usable parameters:** 250 Hz carrier, 6 Hz beat, 65 dB SPL, 10-30 min; expect effects only while the sound plays; consider limiting any single theta block to ~10-20 min and ending with an alpha/beta ramp to avoid the fatigue/confusion seen at 30 min.
- **Notes:** Small sample, no multiple-comparison correction, single session.
- **Source:** Jirakittayakorn N, Wongsawat Y (2017) Front Neurosci 11:365, doi 10.3389/fnins.2017.00365. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2017.00365/full

### 40 Hz gamma binaural beat and working memory: Jirakittayakorn & Wongsawat 2017

- **Evidence level:** moderate
- **Finding:** A 40 Hz binaural beat produced increased gamma-range activity in frontal, temporal and central regions within 15 min of a 30-min listening session; in a second experiment, 20 min of listening improved recall of the working-memory portion of a word list and shifted Brunel Mood Scale scores.
- **Usable parameters:** 40 Hz beat (carrier in the 200-400 Hz range), 15-20 min; place it where the track wants alert, integrative cognition rather than deep relaxation.
- **Notes:** The journal is Int J Psychophysiology, not Clinical Neurophysiology.
- **Source:** Jirakittayakorn N, Wongsawat Y (2017) Int J Psychophysiol 120:96-107, doi 10.1016/j.ijpsycho.2017.07.010, PMID 28739482.

### Gamma/alpha beats and creativity: Reedijk, Bolders & Hommel 2013

- **Evidence level:** weak
- **Finding:** 24 Leiden students heard a 340 Hz carrier with alpha (335/345 Hz = 10 Hz) or gamma (320/360 Hz = 40 Hz) beats, or a constant 340 Hz control, for 3 min before and then during creativity tasks. No overall effect; with spontaneous eye-blink rate (dopamine proxy) as covariate, low-blink participants (<20 blinks/min) benefited on divergent thinking (AUT flexibility), high-blink participants did not or were impaired; convergent thinking (RAT) was unaffected.
- **Usable parameters:** 340 Hz carrier; 10 Hz and 40 Hz beats; 3 min pre-exposure then continuous; effects are individual-difference dependent, so the guide should present creativity benefits as 'possible for some listeners'.
- **Source:** Reedijk SA, Bolders A, Hommel B (2013) Front Hum Neurosci 7:786. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2013.00786/full

### 40 Hz beats and attention: Reedijk et al. 2015 and 2020 follow-up

- **Evidence level:** moderate
- **Finding:** Reedijk, Bolders, Colzato & Hommel 2015 (Front Psychiatry 6:82) found 'high-frequency' (gamma, 40 Hz) binaural beats eliminated the attentional blink, but only in low eye-blink-rate individuals. A 2020 Scientific Reports replication compared 40 Hz vs 16 Hz beats and found 40 Hz produced stronger gamma entrainment and enhanced training-related reduction of the attentional blink; a global-local task study found gamma beats reduced global precedence rather than the blink.
- **Usable parameters:** 40 Hz beat on a ~340-400 Hz carrier for focused-attention segments; 16 Hz beta beat as a milder alternative.
- **Source:** https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2015.00082/full ; https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-020-63980-y

### Beta vs theta/delta beats and vigilance: Lane et al. 1998

- **Evidence level:** moderate
- **Finding:** 29 participants did a 30-min visual vigilance task on three days with pink noise containing either beta (16 and 24 Hz) or theta/delta (1.5 and 4 Hz) binaural beats or simple tones, blind to condition. Beta beats produced more correct detections, fewer false alarms and less negative mood than theta/delta beats, which degraded vigilance.
- **Usable parameters:** Beta beats 16-24 Hz for alert segments; 1.5-4 Hz delta/theta beats only for segments where drowsiness is acceptable; this is the evidence basis for 'never listen while driving or operating machinery'.
- **Notes:** Monroe Institute-style pink-noise bed was used, so pink noise does not block the effect.
- **Source:** Lane JD, Kasian SJ, Owens JE, Marsh GR (1998) Physiol Behav 63(2):249-252, doi 10.1016/s0031-9384(97)00436-8. https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/681333

### Clinical anxiety trial: Padmanabhan, Hildreth & Laws 2005

- **Evidence level:** moderate
- **Finding:** RCT in day-surgery patients (groups 35/34/35): state anxiety (STAI-S) fell 26.3% (95% CI 19-33%) with binaural-beat audio, 11.1% with the same audio without beats, and 3.8% with no intervention.
- **Usable parameters:** Delta/theta-range beats in a relaxing audio bed, ~30 min pre-event; the most replicated real-world use of binaural beats is acute anxiety reduction.
- **Notes:** Later trials in bronchoscopy, cataract and dental settings show smaller or mixed effects.
- **Source:** Padmanabhan R, Hildreth AJ, Laws D (2005) Anaesthesia 60:874-877, doi 10.1111/j.1365-2044.2005.04287.x. https://associationofanaesthetists-publications.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1365-2044.2005.04287.x

### Long-term daily delta beats: Wahbeh, Calabrese & Zwickey 2007

- **Evidence level:** weak
- **Finding:** Uncontrolled pilot: 8 healthy adults listened daily for 60 days to a CD with delta-frequency binaural beats; trait anxiety decreased (p = 0.004), quality of life increased, IGF-1 and dopamine decreased. Authors call for a larger RCT.
- **Usable parameters:** Delta (1-4 Hz) beats, daily use for 2 months; useful precedent for a 'practice daily for 60 days' instruction, but evidence is n = 8 and uncontrolled.
- **Notes:** A companion Wahbeh 2007 paper (JACM 13:199-206) covers EEG/neuropsychological effects.
- **Source:** Wahbeh H, Calabrese C, Zwickey H (2007) J Altern Complement Med 13(1):25-32, doi 10.1089/acm.2006.6196, PMID 17309374.

### Delta/theta tapes for anxiety: Le Scouarnec et al. 2001

- **Evidence level:** weak
- **Finding:** 15 mildly anxious volunteers listened to delta/theta binaural-beat tapes for 4 weeks (10-17 listening sessions total); daily diary anxiety ratings fell significantly, but later reviewers call the other measures slight/non-significant; single-group design.
- **Usable parameters:** Delta/theta beats, ~2-4 sessions per week, 4 weeks.
- **Source:** Le Scouarnec RP et al. (2001) Altern Ther Health Med 7(1):58-63, PMID 11191043. https://www.unboundmedicine.com/medline/citation/11191043

### Meditation facilitation 7 Hz vs 15 Hz: Lavallee, Koren & Persinger 2011

- **Evidence level:** weak
- **Finding:** 15 min of a 7 Hz binaural beat increased left temporal delta power and was rated 'facilitative' of meditation; 15 Hz increased gamma power and was 'hindering'; experienced meditators (>10 months) showed markedly higher theta with the 7 Hz beat, novices showed elevated theta but no difference between beats.
- **Usable parameters:** 7 Hz beat for the meditation core; avoid 15 Hz beats during the meditative segments; 15 min blocks.
- **Notes:** Persinger-lab study; small n.
- **Source:** Lavallee CF, Koren SA, Persinger MA (2011) J Altern Complement Med 17(4):351-355, doi 10.1089/acm.2009.0691.

### Beta beats and memory: Kennerly 1994

- **Evidence level:** weak
- **Finding:** Master's thesis (West Georgia College): beta-frequency binaural beats mixed with music improved memory-span tasks vs control, but carrier frequency and session duration were not reported, limiting reuse.
- **Usable parameters:** Beta (~15-20 Hz) beats under music for learning segments; treat as a historical pointer only.
- **Source:** Kennerly RC (1994) An empirical investigation into the effect of beta frequency binaural beat audio signals on four measures of human memory. MA thesis, West Georgia College; cited in Orozco Perez 2020 and Beauchene 2016 (PLoS ONE 11:e0166630).

### Monaural beats reduce state anxiety: Chaieb et al. 2017

- **Evidence level:** moderate
- **Finding:** 25 healthy adults heard 5-min blocks of monaural beats at 6 Hz (107/113 -> 217/223 Hz), 10 Hz (105/115 -> 215/225 Hz), 40 Hz (90/130 -> 200/240 Hz) or a pure-tone control, with carriers sweeping 110-220 Hz over 1 min cycles. All three beat conditions lowered STAI-S state anxiety vs control (36.2 -> 33.4 / 33.0 / 33.4); no effects on long-term memory, working memory or vigilance.
- **Usable parameters:** Monaural beats at 6, 10 or 40 Hz on 110-220 Hz carriers; as little as 5 min lowers state anxiety; monaural beats work over speakers (no headphones required) unlike binaural beats.
- **Source:** Chaieb L, Wilpert EC, Hoppe C, Axmacher N, Fell J (2017) Front Hum Neurosci 11:251, doi 10.3389/fnhum.2017.00251. https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2017.00251/full

### Isochronic/periodic click and drum entrainment: Will & Berg 2007

- **Evidence level:** moderate
- **Finding:** EEG responses to periodic drum sounds and clicks at 1-8 Hz vs silence and random noise: periodic stimulation produced a tonic synchronization response in the delta range peaking at 2 Hz, a phasic theta response, and increased phase synchronization across 13-44 Hz (mainly lower gamma) modulated by the stimulus period.
- **Usable parameters:** Isochronic pulses or drum hits at 1-8 Hz; 2 Hz (120 bpm) gives the strongest tonic delta synchronization; rhythmic pulses engage beta/gamma phase-locking more reliably than binaural beats.
- **Source:** Will U, Berg E (2007) Brain wave synchronization and entrainment to periodic acoustic stimuli. Neurosci Lett 424(1):55-60, doi 10.1016/j.neulet.2007.07.036. https://www.researchgate.net/publication/6131130

### Auditory steady-state response peaks near 40 Hz

- **Evidence level:** **strong**
- **Finding:** Galambos, Makeig & Talmachoff 1981 showed that clicks repeated at ~40/s summate into a stable '40-Hz event-related potential' (3-4 cycles of 40 Hz per click), the basis of the ASSR; later ASSR literature (Picton et al. 2003 review, Int J Audiol) reports the response amplitude is largest for modulation rates near 40 Hz in awake adults, while 80-100 Hz rates are used for sleeping subjects and infants. Orozco Perez 2020 likewise found the 40 Hz monaural condition gave the highest SSR peak.
- **Usable parameters:** For the most robust measurable cortical following, use 40 Hz isochronic clicks or 40 Hz amplitude modulation (e.g. 1 ms clicks every 25 ms) rather than a 40 Hz binaural beat.
- **Source:** Galambos R, Makeig S, Talmachoff PJ (1981) PNAS 78(4):2643-2647 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC319406 ; Picton TW, John MS, Dimitrijevic A, Purcell D (2003) Int J Audiol 42:177-219 (40 Hz-vs-80 Hz detail from the review; not re-verified online in this session)

### Isochronic tones: evidence base

- **Evidence level:** weak
- **Finding:** Direct peer-reviewed evidence for isochronic tones is thin: a 2021 literature review of 2009-2019 RCTs found 17 Jadad>=3 trials, of which 15 used binaural beats and only 2 used isochronic tones; Galvez et al. 2017 (25 young adults, randomized, double-blind) used a single progressive session of isochronic tones at 6, 10 and 40 Hz and reported reduced anxiety; '10 Hz isochronic tones for 5 min increased neurocognitive focus' appears in six reviewed studies. No head-to-head EEG trial of isochronic vs binaural vs monaural was found.
- **Usable parameters:** Isochronic pulses at 6, 10 or 40 Hz, 5 min blocks; the mechanism (a true amplitude-modulated stimulus) is the same one that drives the ASSR, so physiological plausibility is higher than for binaural beats even though trials are few.
- **Source:** Effects of binaural beats and isochronic tones on brain wave modulation: literature review. Rev Mex Neurocienc 2021;22(6):238. https://www.scielo.org.mx/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S1665-50442021000600238

### Auditory driving by drumming: Neher 1961/1962

- **Evidence level:** moderate
- **Finding:** Neher recorded scalp EEG in about 10 normal volunteers during drum-like stimulation at 3, 4, 6 and 8 beats/s (40 s on / 40 s off sequences, plus 4 min continuous at 4 beats/s) and observed 'driving' responses at the stimulus frequency and harmonics, mainly over auditory cortex (EEG Clin Neurophysiol 1961;13:449-451). His 1962 Human Biology paper (34:151-160) argued ceremonial drumming works by this mechanism and cited ethnographic drum rates of 7-9 beats/s; the '4-7 Hz theta' summary is a later gloss by reviewers (Maxfield 1990 etc.). He never claimed to induce full ritual trance in the lab.
- **Usable parameters:** Drum pulses at 3-8 Hz (180-480 bpm); 4 Hz (240 bpm) is the rate Neher used for continuous driving; 40-s stimulation / 40-s rest cycles are a documented structure.
- **Notes:** Year of the EEG paper is 1961, not 1962.
- **Source:** https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc6a96df126065038a599debc41112c41a8db681 ; review 'The rhythms of trance' Neurosci Biobehav Rev 2026 https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0149763426001636

### Shamanic drumming tempo and theta: Maxfield 1990

- **Evidence level:** weak
- **Finding:** Melinda Maxfield's Institute of Transpersonal Psychology dissertation (12 participants) compared a shamanic drumming tape at ~4-4.5 beats/s, an I Ching tape at 3-4 beats/s and free drumming; the 4-4.5 beats/s pattern was 'the most inducting for theta gain', with increases also in alpha and beta. A later replication noted theta was not registered during baseline or stimulus in some analyses.
- **Usable parameters:** Shamanic journey drumming at 4-4.5 Hz (240-270 bpm), monotonous, 15-30 min.
- **Source:** Maxfield MC (1990) Effects of rhythmic drumming on EEG and subjective experience. PhD dissertation, ITP (ProQuest 303885457). Abstract: https://altered-states.net/barry/update235/Effects%20of%20Rhythmic%20Drumming%20on%20EEG%20and%20Subjective%20Experience%20.pdf

### Harner / Foundation for Shamanic Studies drumming protocol

- **Evidence level:** traditional (a lineage's own teaching, not a tested claim)
- **Finding:** The FSS site states the journey drum is 'played in a steady rhythm at approximately 5 beats per second' and offers 15-min and 30-min solo drum recordings that end with a fast 'callback' rhythm. Gingras et al. 2014 measured Harner's own recording at 254.7 bpm (4.2 Hz) while citing the literature figure of 220 bpm (~3.7 Hz). The widely quoted '205-220 bpm' therefore corresponds to the low end (3.4-3.7 Hz) and the measured practice is nearer 4.2-5 Hz.
- **Usable parameters:** Monotonous frame-drum at 220-270 bpm (3.7-4.5 Hz) for 15-30 min; end with a callback: a short burst of very fast drumming (~7-8 Hz / 420-480 bpm for ~10-30 s), a pause, then four groups of fast beats, to signal return.
- **Source:** https://www.shamanism.org/drumming/ ; Gingras B, Pohler G, Fitch WT (2014) PLoS ONE 9(7):e102103 https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0102103

### Drumming physiology: Gingras, Pohler & Fitch 2014

- **Evidence level:** **strong**
- **Finding:** 39 novices listened for 15 min lying down to either repetitive drumming or instrumental meditation music, with shamanic-journey or relaxation instructions. Salivary cortisol fell in all conditions with no extra drop for drumming; shamanic instructions produced distinct subjective experiences (imagery, altered time). Conclusion: the subjective effects of journeying are not reflected in a differential endocrine response.
- **Usable parameters:** 15 min of monotonous drumming while lying down is enough to lower cortisol; explicit journey instructions (set/setting) drive the 'otherworld' phenomenology more than the sound itself.
- **Source:** Gingras B, Pohler G, Fitch WT (2014) PLoS ONE 9(7):e102103. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4085008/

### Gamma in long-term meditators: Lutz et al. 2004

- **Evidence level:** **strong**
- **Finding:** Eight long-term Tibetan Buddhist practitioners (10,000-50,000 h of practice) self-induced sustained high-amplitude gamma oscillations (25-42 Hz) and long-range phase synchrony during non-referential compassion meditation, especially over lateral frontoparietal electrodes; they also had a higher gamma-to-slow-wave ratio at rest that increased during practice and remained elevated afterwards.
- **Usable parameters:** Gamma band of interest 25-42 Hz (centre ~40 Hz); this is a correlate of trained meditation, not evidence that an external 40 Hz tone creates the state.
- **Source:** Lutz A, Greischar LL, Rawlings NB, Ricard M, Davidson RJ (2004) PNAS 101(46):16369-16373, doi 10.1073/pnas.0407401101. https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0407401101

### 40 Hz sensory stimulation (GENUS) in mice: Iaccarino 2016, Martorell 2019

- **Evidence level:** moderate
- **Finding:** Iaccarino et al. (Nature 2016) found 1 h of 40 Hz light flicker reduced Abeta1-40 and Abeta1-42 in mouse visual cortex by ~58% and altered microglia, but only in visual cortex. Martorell et al. (Cell 2019) delivered auditory 40 Hz stimulation (10 kHz tone pulses at 40 Hz, 1 h/day for 7 days) and found gamma entrainment in auditory cortex and hippocampus with reduced amyloid and improved spatial/recognition memory in 5XFAD mice; combined audio-visual stimulation spread the effect to prefrontal cortex. A 2023 study failed to reproduce amyloid reduction with chronic 24/40/80 Hz visual flicker in 5XFAD mice.
- **Usable parameters:** 40 Hz click/tone train, 1 h per day; the pulse structure that works in the lab is a short tone burst repeated every 25 ms.
- **Notes:** Animal data; 'GENUS' = Gamma ENtrainment Using Sensory stimuli (MIT Tsai lab).
- **Source:** Iaccarino HF et al. (2016) Nature 540:230-235 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27929004/ ; Martorell AJ et al. (2019) Cell 177:256-271, doi 10.1016/j.cell.2019.02.014 https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/132749

### 40 Hz stimulation in humans: safety and feasibility (Chan et al. 2022; 2025 extension)

- **Evidence level:** moderate
- **Finding:** Chan et al. (PLoS ONE 2022;17(12):e0278412): 40 Hz light (390-400 lux) plus 40 Hz clicks (1 ms pulses every 25 ms, 4% duty cycle, 68 dB) was safe and induced entrainment in cortex, hippocampus and amygdala in a single session (13 young, 12 older adults, 16 mild AD, 2 epilepsy patients with no epileptiform activity); in a 3-month randomized pilot (n = 15, 1 h daily) adverse events did not differ from control (dry eye, light sensitivity, nervousness, drowsiness) and compliance was ~87-91%. A 2025 open-label extension reported no adverse effects over 2 years of 1 h daily use in 5 patients.
- **Usable parameters:** 40 Hz click train: 1 ms clicks at 25 ms intervals, ~68 dB SPL, up to 60 min/day; this is the best-documented safe dose for a 40 Hz auditory segment.
- **Notes:** Human efficacy for cognition remains unproven; the relevance here is safety and dosing.
- **Source:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9714926/ ; Chan D et al. (2025) Alzheimer's & Dementia, doi 10.1002/alz.70792 https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41137616/

### Pineal gland: what is actually known

- **Evidence level:** **strong**
- **Finding:** The adult pineal weighs <0.2 g and secretes ~30 ug/day of melatonin, regulating circadian rhythm. Calcification is physiological and age-related: pooled prevalence 61.65% (95% CI 52.8-70.5) in a 2023 meta-analysis; 71.6% in 11,941 brain CTs; ~1% under age 6, 39% at 8-14 years. No study has shown that any sound frequency changes pineal structure, calcification or melatonin output.
- **Usable parameters:** None for sound. The only acoustic/pineal link with any evidence is indirect: evening light exposure suppresses melatonin, so a night-time listening session should be done with dim/red light.
- **Notes:** The evidence level is 'strong' for the anatomy, and 'none' for the claim that audio opens or decalcifies the gland.
- **Source:** Prevalence of pineal gland calcification: systematic review and meta-analysis (2023) https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9987140/ ; Age and gender related prevalence of intracranial calcifications in 12,000 subjects https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0891061816300783 ; Nichols DE (2018) J Psychopharmacol 32(1):30-36

### DMT and the pineal gland

- **Evidence level:** weak
- **Finding:** Barker et al. 2013 detected DMT in pineal microdialysate of living rats; Dean et al. 2019 (Sci Rep) found DMT and its synthesising enzymes in rat cortex and hippocampus even after pinealectomy, at levels comparable to other monoamines in some conditions. Nichols 2018 concludes the pineal is far too small to make psychoactive quantities, and no human pineal DMT has been measured; a 2026 replication attempt in four rat regions gave conflicting results.
- **Usable parameters:** None; the 'spirit molecule' pineal narrative (Strassman 2001) is a hypothesis with no human support and should be labelled as such.
- **Source:** Barker SA et al. (2013) Biomed Chromatogr 27:1690-1700 https://analyticalsciencejournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/bmc.2981 ; Dean JG et al. (2019) Sci Rep 9:9333 https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-45812-w ; Nichols DE (2018) https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0269881117736919

### Origin of the '963 Hz / 936 Hz pineal frequency' claims

- **Evidence level:** none (no evidence)
- **Finding:** 963 Hz is the top tone of the nine 'Solfeggio frequencies'. The six core tones (396, 417, 528, 639, 741, 852) were derived by Joseph Puleo (1970s) from a numerological reduction of verse numbers in the Book of Numbers and published with Leonard Horowitz in 'Healing Codes for the Biological Apocalypse' (1999); 174, 285 and 963 Hz were added later by Horowitz. The medieval 'solfeggio' of Guido d'Arezzo was a set of sight-singing syllables with no Hz values. 936 Hz is a later, undocumented variant of 963 Hz circulated on YouTube/Spotify titles. No peer-reviewed study has tested either tone on the pineal gland.
- **Usable parameters:** If used, 963 Hz (and 852 Hz, 'third eye') can be included as a tuning/aesthetic choice; note that at 963 Hz a binaural beat is at the edge of perceptibility (keep any beat on it <= 6-8 Hz, or use it as a plain drone/monaural beat instead).
- **Notes:** Guide should describe these as 20th-century numerology, not ancient or physiological.
- **Source:** Horowitz LG, Puleo J (1999) Healing Codes for the Biological Apocalypse (Tetrahedron). Summaries: https://scienceinsights.org/what-is-963-hz-solfeggio-frequency-claims-science/ ; https://sine-immersive.com/blog/solfeggio-frequencies-explained

### Brainwave bands used by entrainment practitioners and their provenance

- **Evidence level:** moderate
- **Finding:** Clinical EEG convention: delta 0.5-4 Hz, theta 4-8 Hz, alpha 8-12/13 Hz, beta 13-30 Hz, gamma 30-100 Hz. SMR (12-15 Hz) was defined by M. Barry Sterman from cat sensorimotor cortex (11-19 Hz band, 12-15 Hz core) and his 1972 epilepsy patient trained to increase 12-15 Hz. 'Epsilon' (<0.5 Hz, 'as slow as one cycle per 40 s') and 'HyperGamma' (~100 Hz) / 'Lambda' (~200 Hz) are terms coined by Jeffrey Thompson (Center for Neuroacoustic Research, Carlsbad CA), attributed by him to 1988-1996 intra-operative and monk recordings; they appear only in his patents and product pages, with no independent peer-reviewed validation.
- **Usable parameters:** Beat/pulse targets: delta 0.5-4, theta 4-8, alpha 8-12, SMR 12-15, beta 13-30, gamma 30-45 (binaural) or 40 (isochronic). 'Epsilon' <0.5 Hz and 'lambda' 100-200 Hz are legitimate as modulation choices (e.g. a 0.1-0.3 Hz slow swell under a 40 Hz pulse) but should be labelled as practitioner terms without validation.
- **Notes:** Band edges vary by author (alpha to 12 or 13 Hz; gamma from 30 or 35 Hz).
- **Source:** Sterman MB, Friar L (1972) EEG Clin Neurophysiol 33:89-95; https://www.ccjm.org/content/77/7_suppl_3/S60.full.pdf ; Thompson patent US8932218B1 https://patents.google.com/patent/US8932218B1/en ; https://scientificsounds.com/product/epsilon/

### The 'Awakened Mind' pattern (Cade, Wise, Mind Mirror)

- **Evidence level:** anecdotal
- **Finding:** C. Maxwell Cade (with Nona Coxhead) published 'The Awakened Mind: Biofeedback and the Development of Higher States of Awareness' (Delacorte, 1979). Using the Mind Mirror dual-hemisphere spectral EEG he mapped >4,000 people, including yogis, Zen masters and healers, and described an optimum pattern combining simultaneous beta, alpha, theta and delta in both hemispheres ('lucid awareness coexistent with thought'). Anna Wise (student for 8 years, brought the Mind Mirror to the US in 1981) argued it is the relationship of the peak frequencies of the four bands that defines it. No independent replication; the pattern is defined qualitatively, not in published amplitude percentages.
- **Usable parameters:** Design implication: layer a low-level alpha (10 Hz) and theta (6 Hz) component with a light beta/gamma pulse rather than a single beat frequency, which is what Wise-style 'awakened mind' audio attempts.
- **Source:** https://archive.org/details/awakenedmindbiof00cade ; Wise A (1995) The High-Performance Mind; Wise A (2002) Awakening the Mind; https://www.themindmirror.com/

### Schumann resonances: measured values and biological-effect evidence

- **Evidence level:** weak
- **Finding:** Measured Earth-ionosphere cavity modes are approximately 7.83 Hz (fundamental), 14.3, 20.8, 27.3 and 33.8 Hz (some sources: 14.1, 20.3, 26.3, 32.5); about eight modes are detectable between 7 and 52 Hz; the fundamental varies by roughly +/-0.5 Hz with daily/seasonal ionospheric height changes and solar activity. Biological claims rest on small studies from one group: Pobachenko et al. 2006 reported real-time coherence between Schumann and EEG spectra in the 6-16 Hz band in a small sample; Saroka & Persinger 2016 (n = 184 QEEGs) found 7-8, 13-14 and 19-20 Hz peaks in some but not all participants. No independent replication; fact-checkers judge '7.83 Hz tuning/healing' claims unsupported.
- **Usable parameters:** 7.83 Hz is a perfectly good theta-range beat/pulse rate (and 14.3 Hz a usable SMR/beta rate); include them for symbolic coherence but label the 'Earth-tuning' idea as unproven.
- **Source:** NASA-hosted review https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/154_48a5766284d47dc2523cb38d856654f6_STOLCVIKTOR.pdf ; Saroka KS, Persinger MA (2016) https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4718669/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schumann_resonances

### Origin of 'frequency-following response' marketing and Hemi-Sync

- **Evidence level:** traditional (a lineage's own teaching, not a tested claim)
- **Finding:** Robert A. Monroe's US patent 3,884,218 ('Method of inducing and maintaining various stages of sleep in the human being', filed 30 Sep 1970, granted 20 May 1975) is the origin of Hemi-Sync; the Monroe Institute's claim that beats are 'evidenced by the EEG frequency-following response' is the institute's own framing (F. Holmes Atwater, J Sci Exploration 1997), not an independently validated mechanism. Hemi-Sync mixes beats with pink sound, surf or music.
- **Usable parameters:** Monroe-style layering: multiple simultaneous beats (e.g. 4 Hz + 10 Hz on different carriers) under pink noise; carriers ~100-400 Hz.
- **Source:** https://hemi-sync.com/research-papers/the-hemi-sync-process/ ; https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/robert-monroe/

### Safety: seizures, adverse effects, hearing, driving

- **Evidence level:** moderate
- **Finding:** No published case report of a seizure induced by binaural beats was found; musicogenic epilepsy is rare (~1 in 10 million; ~110 published cases 1884-2007); 40 Hz sensory-stimulation trials included epilepsy patients with no epileptiform activity, yet research trials still routinely exclude people with seizure history, auditory hypersensitivity or prior adverse reactions. Reported side effects are anecdotal (headache, ear fatigue, dizziness, anxiety, mostly at high volume or long sessions); one controlled study (6 Hz, 30 min) found increased confusion/fatigue/depression items. Hearing-safety limits: WHO/ITU H.870 80 dBA for 40 h/week (75 dBA for sensitive users); NIOSH 85 dBA for 8 h with a 3 dB exchange rate. Theta/delta beats degrade vigilance (Lane 1998), so no listening while driving.
- **Usable parameters:** Master the track so a typical listen is ~60-70 dB SPL (the research range: 65-70 dB SPL); cap peak level; keep any single deep-theta/delta block to ~10-20 min; give an explicit alpha/beta ramp before the end; warn: not for epilepsy, not while driving, stop if headache/ear ringing.
- **Source:** Wired for sound: the effect of sound on the epileptic brain. Seizure 2022;102:22-31 https://www.seizure-journal.com/article/S1059-1311(22)00214-X/fulltext ; https://hearingreview.com/hearing-loss/hearing-loss-prevention/risk-factors/standards-for-safe-listening-past-present-and-future ; Chan 2022 PLoS ONE

### Headphone requirement and interaural level

- **Evidence level:** **strong**
- **Finding:** Binaural beats are generated centrally (superior olivary complex) from the phase difference between the two ears, so they require stereo headphones with each ear receiving its own tone; monaural beats and isochronic tones are acoustic amplitude modulations and work over speakers. The beat percept survives moderate interaural intensity differences (Oster) but not a sub-threshold ear (Gu et al. 1995).
- **Usable parameters:** Instruct headphone use for binaural sections; keep L/R within ~3 dB; put monaural/isochronic layers in both channels so the track still 'works' on speakers.
- **Source:** Oster 1973; Orozco Perez 2020 eNeuro (brainstem FFR section); Grose, Buss & Hall 2012

### Design recommendations from this lens

- Carrier tones: keep every binaural-beat carrier between ~200 and 450 Hz (sweet spot 400-440 Hz, Oster/Licklider/Schwarz); never above ~1000 Hz for a binaural beat. Research carriers that worked: 250 Hz (Jirakittayakorn), 340 Hz (Reedijk), ~400 Hz (Orozco Perez, Schwarz & Taylor).
- Beat rates: binaural beats are most salient at 4-15 Hz and effectively vanish above ~30 Hz, so deliver any 40 Hz gamma segment as monaural beats or isochronic clicks (1 ms click every 25 ms, as in Chan 2022/Martorell 2019) rather than a 40 Hz binaural beat.
- Session shape (45-60 min): (1) 3-5 min alpha 10 Hz settling (pre-exposure matters, Garcia-Argibay); (2) 10-20 min theta 6-7.83 Hz core (theta rises within ~10 min, Jirakittayakorn; 7 Hz facilitates meditation, Lavallee); (3) optional 10-15 min drumming/isochronic layer at 4-4.5 Hz (240-270 bpm; Maxfield, Harner) for the 'journey' segment, ending with a fast callback; (4) 10-15 min 40 Hz isochronic/monaural gamma for integration and alertness (20 min improved working-memory recall; Lutz gamma 25-42 Hz); (5) 5 min ramp back through SMR 12-15 Hz / beta 16 Hz so the listener ends alert (Lane 1998 beta improves vigilance and mood).
- Keep each stimulus block at or above 8-10 min (the measurable-response and meta-analytic duration thresholds), but limit continuous deep theta/delta to ~20 min, since the only controlled 30-min 6 Hz exposure raised confusion/fatigue scores.
- Level: target ~65-70 dB SPL at the ear for the beat layers (the level used in the EEG studies) and master so the whole track stays well under WHO's 80 dBA weekly guideline; keep left/right within ~3 dB.
- Background: a gentle pink-noise or surf bed is acceptable (used by Lane 1998 and Hemi-Sync) and may help the percept, but do not bury beats under dense music, since beats-alone outperformed beats-in-music in the 2019 meta-analysis.
- Redundancy: layer binaural beat + a quieter monaural/isochronic pulse at the same rate so the track degrades gracefully on speakers and gives the stronger ASSR-type following (Orozco Perez; Will & Berg).
- Symbolic frequencies: 7.83 Hz (Schumann) is a legitimate theta beat rate and 14.3 Hz a legitimate SMR rate; 963/852 Hz Solfeggio tones can be used as drones or monaural beats, but the guide must label them as numerological (Puleo/Horowitz 1999) with no pineal evidence.
- Include a 'journey' instruction script (set and setting): Gingras 2014 shows the phenomenology of shamanic journeying comes from the instructions as much as the sound.
- Provide usage guidance: headphones required for binaural sections, lying or seated, eyes closed, dim or red light if used at night (melatonin), daily use for 4-8 weeks is the pattern in the few longitudinal pilots (Le Scouarnec 2001, Wahbeh 2007).

### Cautions from this lens

- Entrainment claims: the best systematic review (Ingendoh 2023) found only 5 of 14 EEG studies supporting binaural-beat 'brainwave entrainment'; the guide must not promise that the track will 'tune' the brain to a frequency.
- Pineal / third-eye claims: no study shows any sound frequency affects the pineal gland, its calcification (present in ~62-72% of adults) or melatonin; human pineal DMT has never been measured. 963/936 Hz 'pineal frequencies' originate in 1999 numerology.
- Dimension/time travel, manifestation and 'control over vibration' are not testable claims; the track can only honestly target relaxation, anxiety reduction, altered-state phenomenology (drumming + instructions) and modest attention effects (g ~0.4-0.45).
- Effects are individual: creativity and attention benefits appeared only in low eye-blink-rate (low-dopamine) individuals; older listeners perceive gamma binaural beats less well.
- Mood can go the wrong way: 30 min of 6 Hz theta increased confusion, fatigue and depression items in the only controlled study; always ramp back to alpha/beta before ending.
- Seizure risk: no published binaural-beat seizure case exists and 40 Hz trials included epilepsy patients safely, but trials still exclude seizure history, so advise anyone with epilepsy, photosensitivity or auditory hypersensitivity to consult a neurologist first.
- Never use while driving or operating machinery: theta/delta beats measurably impair vigilance (Lane 1998).
- Hearing: respect WHO 80 dBA/40 h per week; stop if ear ringing or headache occurs; keep both channels audible (sub-threshold-ear beats do not work).
- Schumann 7.83 Hz 'Earth tuning' and Thompson's 'epsilon/lambda' bands are practitioner terms with no independent replication; present them as such.
- Several widely repeated citation details are wrong in popular sources: Licklider et al. is 1950 (not 1957); Neher's EEG paper is 1961; Jirakittayakorn's 40 Hz paper is Int J Psychophysiology; Harner's drumming is ~4.2-5 beats/s, not 205-220 bpm.

## 2. Eastern, Middle-Eastern and classical-Western sacred sound

*Lens scope:* Eastern, Middle-Eastern and classical-Western sacred sound traditions (Hindu/Vedic, Tibetan Buddhist, Chinese/Taoist, Japanese/Korean/Vietnamese, Sufi/Islamic, Jewish Kabbalah/Merkabah, Christian/Greek/Hermetic, Zoroastrian/Sikh/Jain/Baha'i) — what each actually prescribes in pitch, rhythm, breath and session shape, with the evidence level for each element.

### Hindu/Vedic — Om tuning at 136.10 Hz (Hans Cousto 'Earth-year tone')

- **Evidence level:** weak
- **Finding:** The '136.1 Hz Om' used by most sound-healers is not an ancient Indian pitch; it is Hans Cousto's 1978 calculation: the sidereal year (365.256 d = 3.16875e-8 Hz) raised 32 octaves = 136.10 Hz, a C# in a tuning where A = 432.1 Hz (31.4 cents below A440). Cousto then asserted it 'falls very close' to the pitch at which Om is chanted and to the Indian tanpura drone. Indian classical Sa is not fixed; it is commonly set near C#/D for male vocalists (C#4 = 138.6 Hz at A440), which is the only sense in which the match holds. The 'Sadja = exactly 136.10 Hz' claim has no primary source.
- **Usable parameters:** Drone fundamental 136.10 Hz (C#); sub-octaves 68.05 Hz and 34.03 Hz; reference A = 432.1 Hz for any harmonic material built on it; Om chant guide pitch for listener ≈ 136 Hz (male) / 272 Hz (female octave).
- **Notes:** Calculation is reproducible (strong); the claim that it is 'the' Om pitch or has physiological effect is Cousto's assertion only.
- **Source:** Hans Cousto, The Cosmic Octave (German 1984; English 1988/2000); Planetware tuning-fork table https://www.planetware.de/octave/The_CoOc_Tuning.pdf ; history https://www.planetware.de/octave/music-and-everything.html ; critique https://www.soundmedicineacademy.com/pages/sound-healing-blog/healing-with-the-planetary-frequencies

### Hindu/Vedic — Nada Yoga: the ten inner sounds (Hamsa Upanishad) and Nada Anusandhana (Hatha Yoga Pradipika ch. 4)

- **Evidence level:** traditional (a lineage's own teaching, not a tested claim)
- **Finding:** Hamsa Upanishad lists ten anahata (unstruck) sounds heard in sequence after prolonged mantra: (1) chini, (2) chini-chini, (3) bell (ghanta), (4) conch (shankha), (5) lute (vina), (6) cymbals (tala), (7) flute (venu), (8) kettledrum (bheri), (9) mridanga, (10) thunder of clouds (megha-garjana); the tenth may be heard without the first nine. Stated purpose: samadhi/laya; HYP 4.29 'nada is the snare that binds the deer of the mind'.
- **Usable parameters:** Sound-design arc of 10 timbres in this order: tinkling → bell → conch → tanpura/vina → cymbal/tingsha → flute → kettledrum → mridanga → thunder; present inner-sound cues panned slightly RIGHT (HYP right-ear instruction); move from loud/dense to quiet/subtle over the session (HYP's loud→subtle progression).
- **Notes:** Texts describe sounds heard INSIDE after practice, not sounds to be played; the track can only imitate them as cues.

### Hindu/Vedic — Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra sound dharanas (Kashmir Shaivism)

### Hindu/Vedic — So'ham / Hamsa ajapa-japa: the 21,600-breath day

- **Evidence level:** traditional (a lineage's own teaching, not a tested claim)
- **Finding:** Hamsa, Dhyanabindu and Yogachudamani Upanishads state the breath repeats the mantra 'Hamsa'/'So'ham' 21,600 times per day-night (60 breaths = 1 prana, 6 pranas = 1 nadika, 60 nadikas = 24 h → 21,600), i.e. a resting rate of 15 breaths/min, 4 s per cycle. Exhale = 'Ha', inhale = 'Sa' (Dhyanabindu) or inhale 'So', exhale 'Ham'. Stated purpose: the breath itself is the mantra of the self (ajapa = 'not repeated by anyone'); some manuscripts give 21,606.
- **Usable parameters:** Opening breath-pacing cue: 4.0 s cycle (15/min) = scriptural 'natural' rate; then slow to 6/min (10 s cycle) for coherence sections. Inhale cue syllable 'So' (rising), exhale 'Ham' (falling, nasal).
- **Notes:** The 21,600 figure is a cosmological ideal (half of the 43,200-based cycle numbers), not a measured rate.
- **Source:** Hamsa Upanishad https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamsa_Upanishad ; Dhyanabindu/Yogacudamani per https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/essay/yoga-upanishads-study/d/doc1598818.html ; calculation https://www.hindu-blog.com/2023/11/ajapajapa-in-hindu-religion-silent.html

### Hindu/Vedic — Bhramari (bee-humming) pranayama

- **Evidence level:** moderate
- **Finding:** HYP 2.68: inhale rapidly with a sound like a male bee, exhale slowly humming like a female bee; 'bliss arises in the heart'. Modern measurements: humming pitch is the practitioner's own fundamental (adult male F0 ≈ 85-155 Hz, female ≈ 165-255 Hz; Manjunatha et al. 2018 recorded mean F0 211→217 Hz in 24 young women after 30 sessions). Humming raises nasal nitric oxide ~15-fold vs quiet exhalation (Weitzberg & Lundberg 2002, n=10). EEG: short hums (≤9 s) showed no significant theta change in most subjects (AIAN 2023); HRV studies report parasympathetic shift during simple humming.
- **Usable parameters:** Guided humming block: 5-9 rounds, inhale 4 s, hum exhale 10-15 s at own pitch; backing drone at 136 Hz (C#) and 272 Hz so most voices find a consonant hum (C#2/C#3/G#3); optional 'bee' texture at 100-250 Hz with slow amplitude flutter (~6 Hz).
- **Notes:** The '120 Hz' figure sometimes cited for Bhramari has no source; the 13 Hz in the EEG paper is a sensor sampling rate, not a pitch.
- **Source:** Weitzberg E, Lundberg JO. Am J Respir Crit Care Med 2002;166:144-145 https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c584ae8986f7092ca03a59700361724d6650538c ; Manjunatha et al. 2018 Evid Based Complement Alternat Med https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2018/4176803 ; EEG study https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10645273/ ; HRV https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10182780/

### Hindu/Tantric — Bija mantras and the Ajna/pineal in Shiva Samhita, Shat-chakra-nirupana, Satyananda

- **Evidence level:** traditional (a lineage's own teaching, not a tested claim)
- **Finding:** Seven-chakra bija set LAM (muladhara) VAM (svadhisthana) RAM (manipura) YAM (anahata) HAM (vishuddha) OM (ajna) and silence/AUM-beyond-sound (sahasrara) comes from Purnananda's Shat-chakra-nirupana (1577) as popularized by Woodroffe (The Serpent Power, 1919). Shiva Samhita ch. 5 describes ajna as a two-petalled lotus between the brows with letters HA and KSHA and the bija OM 'brilliant as the autumn moon'; the yogi who contemplates it 'is not reborn' and gains the fruits of all practices; siddhis (powers) are listed generally in the same chapter with warnings. The identification of ajna with the pineal gland is modern (Satyananda, Kundalini Tantra 1984; Yogananda's 'spiritual eye'/Kutastha between the brows, medulla as 'mouth of God'); classical texts never mention the pineal.
- **Usable parameters:** Chant sequence LAM-VAM-RAM-YAM-HAM-OM, each ~8-10 s, 3-7 repetitions, ascending pitch (practitioners typically step up a scale degree per chakra); finish ajna section with OM then silence (sahasrara). Visual/verbal cue: 'between the eyebrows, two-petalled, bright as the autumn moon'.
- **Notes:** No text assigns Hz to chakras; pitch-stepping is a modern convention.
- **Source:** Shiva Samhita tr. Rai Bahadur Srisa Chandra Vasu (1914) https://ia903209.us.archive.org/29/items/TheSivaSamhita/TheSivaSamhita.pdf ; Woodroffe, The Serpent Power (1919); Satyananda Saraswati, Kundalini Tantra (Bihar School of Yoga 1984); Yogananda AUM technique http://yogananda.com.au/pyr/pyr_aum.html ; Ajna overview https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajna

### Modern chakra-to-frequency tables (used by practitioners) — Goldman just-intonation set, Solfeggio set, Cousto planetary set

### Tibetan Buddhist — Singing bowls: measured acoustics and the one EEG study

- **Evidence level:** moderate
- **Finding:** Bowls have inharmonic partials; each mode is a near-degenerate pair split by a few Hz by wall asymmetry, producing audible beating of ~2-10 Hz (the 'wobble'), plus beating tied to the puja (striker) rotation rate when rubbed (Inácio, Henrique & Antunes 2006 model; mode frequencies scale ≈ n²). The only EEG study (2023, n=17, F3/F4, 5 min) used ONE bowl with fundamental 482.61 Hz and a 6.68 Hz beat and found delta/theta power rising and alpha/beta/gamma falling over 5 minutes. Fundamentals of common bowls span roughly 100-600 Hz (vendor spectra show e.g. 176.5, 289.1, 482.6 Hz).
- **Usable parameters:** Use real bowl samples or additive synthesis with mode pairs split 3-8 Hz (e.g. 136.1 & 142.5 Hz → 6.4 Hz beat; 272.2 & 278.9 Hz → 6.7 Hz theta-range beat); fundamentals chosen near C#3 (138.6), G#3 (207.7), C#4 (277.2) to sit in the drone; strike every 20-40 s; rubbed sustain 30-60 s.
- **Notes:** Beating in bowls is monaural (acoustic) and so reaches both ears — a different mechanism from headphone binaural beats; EEG evidence is one small study.
- **Source:** Inácio O, Henrique LL, Antunes J, 'The Dynamics of Tibetan Singing Bowls', Acta Acustica 2006 http://projects.itn.pt/VibroImpacts/Ref_A.pdf ; Terwagne & Bush 2011 arXiv:1106.5657 (withdrawn) ; EEG: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10298245/ ; physical model https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~carmenng/250b/icmc2002.pdf

### Design recommendations from this lens

- Tonal centre: build the whole track on 136.10 Hz (C#, Cousto 'Earth-year'/'Om') with sub-octaves 68.05 and 34.03 Hz and a vocal ison at 138.6/69.3 Hz; it is the one pitch that most practitioner traditions (Cousto forks, planet gongs, 'Om tuning' tanpuras) share, and the bonshō (137.8 Hz) and Gyuto sub-fundamentals (61.7-68 Hz) sit near it. State honestly in the guide that the choice is conventional, not ancient.
- Use pure-ratio intervals from that centre (3:2 = 204.15, 4:3 = 181.47, 9:8 = 153.11, 5:4 = 170.13, 2:1 = 272.2 Hz) so Pythagorean/Timaeus/Kepler material, the Chinese 3:2 lü chain, and shōmyō pentatonics all stay consonant; avoid the Solfeggio set, which clashes with everything else.
- Session architecture (45-60 min): fuse Yoga Nidra's 8 stages with the Mevlevi four-selam plateau shape and the Hekhalot seven-gate ascent: (1) harae/shofar-call awakening 0-3 min; (2) sankalpa/intention 3-5; (3) 61-point body rotation 5-16 with 4-s breath (So'ham 15/min); (4) breath-prayer slowdown to 6/min (Jesus Prayer / Allah-Hu split) 16-26; (5) Six Healing Sounds or Bhramari vocal block 26-33; (6) seven-vowel/seven-planet ascent (Greek vowels on Cousto or ratio tones) 33-40; (7) Bardo 'thousand thunders' sound-light-rays climax with recognition cue 40-44; (8) return to clear-light drone, anhad/nada subtle sounds (bell→flute→silence) 44-52; (9) sankalpa repeated, externalization 52-56; (10) 108-second silence tail.
- Carry the beat frequency acoustically as well as binaurally: layer singing-bowl-style near-degenerate mode pairs split 3-8 Hz (e.g. 136.1/142.5 Hz; 272.2/278.9 Hz) and a damaru/sistrum pulse at 4-6 Hz, so the theta-range modulation survives speaker playback and matches the one EEG result on bowls (6.68 Hz beat → delta/theta rise over 5 min).
- Timbre arc from the Hamsa Upanishad / Inayat Khan nada lists: tinkling → bell → conch → tanpura/vina → cymbal (tingsha) → flute (ney/shakuhachi) → kettledrum → mridanga → thunder, then reverse to subtle; pan inner-sound cues slightly right (HYP right-ear instruction) and let plucked tones decay fully into silence (Vijnana Bhairava v.41).
- Breath pacing is where the real, measurable effects live: 15/min (4 s) early → 6/min (10 s) for the middle 20 minutes (Hesychast/dhikr split: ~4-5 s in, 5-6 s out) → optional short vase-breath holds (5 in / 10-15 hold / 8 out) → Bhramari hums (10-15 s exhale). Give the listener audible cues to hum/chant OM and the bija sequence themselves, because the fMRI/NO/EEG findings are for self-produced sound, not listening.

- Guide text: present ajna/pineal, Ni Wan/Crystal Palace, dasam duar, Ogdoad and bardo language as the traditions' own frames; describe sankalpa (Yoga Nidra) as the historical 'manifestation' technique; attribute the vibration/frequency vocabulary to the Kybalion (1908) and Cousto (1978); mark every Hz table as modern (Goldman 1980s-90s, Solfeggio 1999, Cousto 1978).

### Cautions from this lens

- No tradition examined assigns a frequency to the pineal gland, and no study shows sound opening or stimulating it; the pineal responds to light via the retina-SCN pathway, not to acoustic vibration. The ajna→pineal identification is 20th-century (Satyananda 1984, Yogananda, Mantak Chia).
- All chakra-Hz tables are modern inventions: the 256-480 Hz just-intonation set (Goldman), the Solfeggio set (Puleo/Horowitz 1999, numerology from Numbers 7), and Cousto's planetary assignments (1978, with chakra pairings added by vendors). None has scriptural or experimental support.
- '136.1 Hz = Om' is Cousto's octave calculation from the sidereal year; Indian Sa is not fixed and ancient texts give no Hz. The 'Sadja = exactly 136.10 Hz' claim has no primary source.
- The human evidence is small and modest: OM fMRI n=12; singing-bowl EEG n=17 with one bowl; Liu Zi Jue EEG n=20; Bhramari/humming NO n=10; tummo n≈10. Effects are relaxation-range (theta/alpha, parasympathetic shift), not 'inhuman levels'; binaural-beat literature generally shows small, inconsistent effects. Nothing supports immediate or guaranteed results, travel between dimensions/times, or control of external 'energy'.
- Tomatis's Benedictine-monastery story is unverifiable (no abbey named, no record, inconsistent frequency bands 2-8 kHz); treat as anecdote.
- Safety: keep peak SPL under ~80-85 dB, especially for the low-frequency thunder climax; avoid rhythmic pulses in the 3-30 Hz range for listeners with photosensitive/audiogenic epilepsy without a warning; no breath-holds (vase breathing, Naqshbandi held-breath dhikr) for pregnancy, cardiovascular or respiratory conditions; the Bardo/Chöd imagery of death, demons and 'thousand thunders' can distress listeners with trauma, psychosis history or panic — give an opt-out cue and never present the climax as literal contact with other beings.
- Cultural/sacred-use issues: Gyuto dbyangs is deity-yoga voice not meant for entertainment, the 72 Names and Shofar are sacred within Jewish practice, Chöd requires empowerment in its tradition, and Sikh teaching explicitly rejects pursuing powers; sample or imitate styles rather than lifting liturgical recordings, and credit traditions in the guide.
- Inharmonic bowls/bells will beat unpleasantly against a sine drone unless their fundamentals are chosen near C#/G#/F#; pick samples (or synthesize modes) within ±10 Hz of 136/204/272 Hz for the main drone, and keep deliberately detuned pairs separate from the tonal bed.
- The Gyuto fundamental (60-75 Hz), dungchen 58 Hz and tingsha '>2 kHz' are from secondary summaries and vendors, not from the primary acoustic papers (which are paywalled or withdrawn); Mevlevi rotation speed has never been measured in RPM; huangzhong's absolute pitch is reconstructed only from the Zeng bells (≈415 Hz).
