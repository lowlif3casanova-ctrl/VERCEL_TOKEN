# Design: every frequency, rate and layer, and where it comes from

This document is the complete specification of the Pineal Gateway session, with
the tradition or paper behind each choice and an honest evidence note. Evidence
tags: **measured** (replicated psychoacoustics or physiology), **moderate**
(peer-reviewed but small or few studies), **weak**, **traditional** (a lineage's
own teaching, used as such), **none** (a modern claim with no support, used only
as ceremony and labelled as such). The full research behind these tags, with
sources, is in `RESEARCH_DOSSIER.md`.

## 1. The tonal system

Everything in the session is built on **136.10 Hz**.

- It is Hans Cousto's "Earth-year" tone (calculated 1978; *Die Kosmische
  Oktave* 1984, English *The Cosmic Octave* 1988): the sidereal year
  (365.256 days) raised 32 octaves. It is the pitch most
  sound-healing lineages use for Om, for planet gongs and for tanpura drones
  (via Berendt's *Nada Brahma*). **Traditional / none**: the "Om = 136.1 Hz"
  identification is a 1978 calculation, not an ancient Indian pitch; Indian Sa is
  not fixed. We use it because it is the one pitch the modern practitioner
  world shares, and because it is C# in a tuning where A = 432.1 Hz, so the
  whole session is natively in "432 tuning" without any further gesture.
- Every interval is a pure ratio from that centre (Pythagoras' monochord, Plato's
  *Timaeus* world-soul scale, the Chinese 3:2 lü chain), so no two sustained
  tones beat harshly:

| 108.88 | 4:5 | stone-chamber drone (Neolithic chambers resonate at 95–120 Hz; Ħal Saflieni ≈ 114 Hz) |
| 136.10 | 1:1 | Om; tanpura; bowls; third binaural carrier |

| 963.00 | — | the one Solfeggio tone, struck once as a bowl, in the clear, in the Return |

The chakra bowl ladder in the Ajna stage is the just-intonation set popularised
by Jonathan Goldman (C256 / D288 / E320 / F341.3 / G384 / A426.7 / B480)
transposed onto the Om octave: **272.2 / 306.2 / 340.3 / 362.9 / 408.3 / 453.7 /
510.4 Hz**. **None** as physiology, **traditional** as practice; it was chosen
over the Solfeggio ladder because the Solfeggio tones (396…963, mostly 111 Hz
apart and in no harmonic relation to one another) clash with a C# drone,
whereas this ladder is consonant with everything else in the session.

## 2. The binaural layer (headphones required)

Three sine carriers, each split as left = f − beat/2, right = f + beat/2, at
exactly equal level, perfectly centred and never sent to reverb (reverb smears
the interaural phase that *is* the beat).

| carrier | gain | note |
|---|---|---|
| 408.30 Hz | 0.17 | primary: clearest beat percept near 400–440 Hz (**measured**: Oster 1973; Licklider 1950 for the frequency limits; Schwarz & Taylor 2005 found the binaural-beat steady-state response only at a 400 Hz carrier) |
| 272.20 Hz | 0.12 | octave of Om; still in the useful 200–500 Hz band |
| 136.10 Hz | 0.10 | the Om fundamental; carries the *second* beat during No-Time and the Return (below) |

Beat rates never exceed 14.1 Hz on the binaural layer (binaural beats fade
above ~30 Hz; the 40 Hz gamma is delivered isochronically instead, see §3).
Every ramp is a raised-cosine glide. Averaged over each glide the outer
carriers move at 0.45–1.5 Hz per minute through the descent and the floor and
at about 1.8 Hz per minute in the final 10 → 14.1 Hz wake-up (a raised cosine
peaks at π/2 times the average rate mid-glide); the Om carrier's 6 → 10 Hz
step at 29:00–31:00 runs at 2 Hz per minute and its last 7.83 → 12.5 Hz rise
(46:00–47:42) at about 2.7 Hz per minute.

### The second beat on the Om carrier

From 31:00 to 37:00 the 136.10 Hz carrier runs a **10 Hz alpha** beat while the
outer carriers run 3.0 → 2.13 Hz. This is Monroe's stacking idea (his 1993
patent layers several simultaneous beats; "mind awake, body asleep" is the
Monroe Institute's practitioner description of Focus 10) and the Cade–Wise
"awakened mind" picture (alpha and theta present together). From 37:00 it
eases from 10 Hz to 7.83 Hz by 40:00 and holds 7.83 Hz until 46:00, so that
from 43:30 it sits as a **theta bridge** under the alpha/SMR return; it then
rises to 12.5 Hz between 46:00 and 47:42, a little behind the outer carriers.
**Traditional**: the Focus-level
stacking is a lineage practice; the awakened-mind pattern is an unreplicated EEG
observation (Cade 1979). What is **measured** is only that both beats produce
their own small steady-state response.

## 3. Isochronic (monaural) layers — these also work on speakers

| layer | carrier | rate | when | why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| theta pulse | 204.15 Hz | follows the beat ladder | 12:30–21:30 and 29:30–37:00 | an amplitude-modulated pulse drives the auditory steady-state response more strongly than a binaural beat (Orozco Perez 2020, Will & Berg 2007); it reinforces the drum plateau and the floor |
| gamma cues | 544.40 Hz | 40 Hz | four 90-second cues at 31:48, 34:18, 36:36 and 41:00 | the ASSR is largest near 40 Hz (Galambos 1981, Picton 2003); 40 Hz stimulation during REM raised dream lucidity (Voss 2014, electrical, 30-second bursts, single study); the monks' gamma (Lutz 2004) is 25–42 Hz. Delivered as four cues rather than a bed: the one human lucidity result used short bursts; the 40 Hz safety data (Chan 2022; Martorell 2019 in mice) are for 1-hour continuous click trains, far more than this session gives to gamma; a continuous pulse would compete with the theta/delta floor; and the lineage treats gamma as a "clarity" signal |

Gate shape: raised-cosine squared (cubed for gamma), so the pulses have no click.

## 4. The drum, the call-back, the thunder

- **Frame drum** 11:30–20:30 at **4.25 Hz (255 bpm)**, unaccented except a
  light every-fourth stroke, humanised by ±7 ms. Synthesised: pitch glide
  1.9× → 1× over 25 ms, two membrane modes (f and 1.59 f), skin-slap noise.
  **Moderate**: Neher 1961/62 (auditory driving at 3–8 Hz), Maxfield 1990
  (4–4.5 Hz most theta-inducing, n = 12), Gingras 2014 (the journey
  phenomenology appears only with journey *instructions*, which is why the
  listening guide includes them).
- **Call-back** at 43:05: four groups of seven sharp strokes, ~30 s of fast
  drumming at 8 Hz, four groups of seven again, then a minute of slow
  re-grounding strokes at 1.2 Hz. The fast passage followed by slow grounding
  is the Foundation for Shamanic Studies practice; the "four sevens" framing is
  a modern practitioner convention, included because listeners know it.
- **Thunder** (distant, band-limited brown noise, never loud): one swell opens
  the Drum Gate at 10:54; three pass at 35:24–36:30 at the floor of No-Time.
  **Traditional**: the Bardo Thödol's *chönyid* bardo, where the natural sound
  of reality "reverberates like a thousand thunders" and the instruction is to
  recognise it as one's own mind and not to fear it.
- **Dungchen swells** (68.05 Hz long-horn pedal tones, 28 s crescendos) at 9:18,
  30:00, 33:30, 36:00. **Traditional / secondary measurement**: a 1.7 m
  dungchen sounds a fundamental near 58 Hz (B♭1); 68.05 Hz is the nearest
  pitch in the session's tonal system.

## 5. Bowls, bell, tingsha, conch

- **Singing bowls** are synthesised from the thin-shell law (Terwagne & Bush
  2011; Inácio, Henrique & Antunes 2006): partials at 1 : 2.83 : 5.42 : 8.77 :
  12.87, each mode a doublet that beats slowly, higher modes decaying faster,
  a 6 ms strike transient and a rim-singing swell. **Measured** acoustics.
  The fundamental doublet of each bowl is split at the beat rate of the plateau
  its stage is heading for (8 Hz for the Threshold bowls under a 10 Hz beat,
  7.83 in the Descent, 4.25 in the Drum Gate, 6 in Ajna, 3 / 2.13 in No-Time
  against the outer carriers, 7.83 in the Crown, 10 in the Return), so bowls
  struck during a glide "wah" at the target rate rather than the instantaneous
  one — the same device as
  Balinese *ombak* paired tuning and the one singing-bowl EEG study (a 6.68 Hz
  bowl beat, delta/theta rising over 5 min, n = 17). This makes the beat
  audible even on speakers.
- **Bonshō** (Japanese temple bell) marks every stage boundary: modes 137.8 /
  301.8 / 378.6 Hz as measured on the Hōjōbō bell, 45 s decay, slow beating.
  Its hum sits 1.7 Hz above the Om and is panned slightly right, following the
  Hatha Yoga Pradipika's instruction to listen for the inner sound in the right
  ear. **Traditional / measured acoustics**.
- **Tingsha** (paired cymbals ~2960 Hz, detuned 9 Hz) at 0:08, 2:00, 21:03,
  37:03 and 47:50 — the "tinkling" (*chini*) that opens the Hamsa Upanishad's
  list of ten inner sounds (the cymbal, *tala*, sits mid-list; thunder closes
  it); here it both opens and closes the session. **Traditional**.
- **Pututu cluster** at 38:36, 40:24 and 42:00: three conch-like tones at
  306.22 / 310.12 / 314.05 Hz beating at 3.9 and 7.83 Hz, with the second mode
  near 2×. Chavín de Huántar's excavated *Strombus* trumpets measure 272–340 Hz
  (Cook et al. 2010) and beat strongly when played together (Kolar 2014).
  **Measured** acoustics; **traditional** as an upper-world call.
- **963 Hz bowl** at 45:20, struck once after the tanpura drone has faded, so it
  rings in the clear and clashes with nothing. **None**: 963 Hz is Leonard
  Horowitz's 1999 extension of Joseph Puleo's numerological "Solfeggio"
  set, marketed as the "pineal / crown / God frequency". It is included as a
  ceremonial gesture for listeners who expect it, and labelled as such. No study
  links it to the pineal gland.

## 6. Drones and chant

- **Tanpura / Om drone** at 136.10 Hz throughout (to 45:00), with the Pa
  string (204.15 Hz, the fifth of the 5-8-8-1 tuning) underneath: 28 and 20
  harmonics whose amplitudes bloom and fade on slow independent cycles (the
  *jivari* effect of a real tanpura bridge). From 30:30 to 37:30 a 0.2 Hz (5-second)
  surge rides on it — Monroe's description of the "vibrational state" as a ring
  sweeping head to toe on a roughly 5-second cycle (**anecdotal**, *Journeys Out
  of the Body*).
- **Gyuto-style chord drone** 4:48–11:30 and 37:30–43:00 at 68.05 Hz: 36
  harmonics with a formant that moves between the 5th and 10th harmonics,
  because Smith, Stevens & Tomlinson (JASA 1967) measured the Gyuto chant's
  "chord" as a 60–75 Hz fundamental with the 5th and 10th harmonics lifted.
  Synthesised, not sampled: *dbyangs* is deity-yoga voice, not performance
  material.
- **Didgeridoo** 12:00–21:00 at 68.05 Hz with a 0.55 Hz circular-breathing pulse
  and a formant that wanders over harmonics 3–11 (about 225–730 Hz), kept low
  so it stays in the drone's overtone register; the real instrument's vocal-tract
  resonances sit higher, at 1–2 kHz (Tarnopolsky et al. 2005, **measured**
  acoustics).
- **Stone-chamber drone** 11:30–21:00 at 108.88 Hz, 80 % reverberant: Jahn,
  Devereux & Ibison (1996) measured 95–120 Hz resonances in six Neolithic
  chambers; Cook, Pajot & Leuchter (2008) reported a 110 Hz-specific EEG shift
  in 30 volunteers (**weak**, unreplicated).
- **Seven-vowel ladder** 21:24–28:36, rising through the pure-ratio steps
  136.10 / 153.11 / 170.13 / 181.47 / 204.15 / 229.67 / 255.19 Hz, one vowel per
  step, 50 s each: **A E Ē I O U Ō** (Α Ε Η Ι Ο Υ Ω), the vowels that Demetrius
  (*On Style* §71) says Egyptian priests sang in succession to hymn the gods,
  that the Greek Magical Papyri lay out in seven-fold patterns, and that the
  Nag Hammadi *Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth* uses to rise past the seven
  planetary spheres — the classic textual model of travel between realms by
  sound. Capped by **I-A-Ō** on the octave. Formant synthesis (three resonators
  per vowel, adult male formants, three detuned voices). **Traditional**.
- **A-OM-HUM** 37:36–42:18: "A" at 272.2 Hz (forehead, white), "OM" at 204.15
  Hz (throat, red), "HUM" at 136.1 Hz (heart, blue), 18 s per round, fifteen
  rounds — the Bön / Dzogchen three-syllable scheme. **Traditional**.
- **Breath pacer**: a soft, band-limited swell starting at 8 breaths per minute
  and easing to 6 per minute (10 s cycle) over the first 3 minutes, holding
  6/min through the Descent, and returning at 6 → 7 per minute in the Return.
  6 breaths
  per minute is the resonance-frequency ("coherent") breathing rate with real
  HRV effects (**moderate**: Lehrer & Gevirtz); it is also the Hesychast Jesus
  Prayer and dhikr split (4–5 s in, 5–6 s out).
- **Pink-noise bed** at −39 dB relative to full scale, independent per ear.
  Monroe's patents put pink noise ≥ 10 dB under the beats; Lane (1998) embedded
  beats in pink noise; the 2019 meta-analysis found added noise unnecessary
  (though not an obstacle: Lane 1998 and Hemi-Sync both use a pink bed).
- **"Air"** 39:00–42:48: a whisper of 3–8 kHz filtered noise (−48 dB). Tomatis
  claimed this band "charges" the cortex; **anecdotal**, included only as a
  texture and labelled as his untested hypothesis.

## 7. Mastering

- Oscillators and instruments computed in float64 and accumulated into float32
  stereo buses; phase-continuous oscillators and stateful noise filters (no
  seams); all fades raised-cosine, a 4 s master fade at the end, 20 Hz
  high-pass, reverb (3.6 s synthetic stone-chamber impulse response) on the
  event bus only.
- Integrated loudness **−16 LUFS** (BS.1770-4), true peak ≤ −1 dBTP (measured
  about −3 dBTP on both sessions; the limiter is never engaged). At a comfortable headphone
  setting this lands the beat layers near the 65–70 dB SPL used in the EEG
  studies and keeps the whole session far below the WHO 80 dBA weekly limit.
- Exports: 48 kHz float32 WAV master, 24-bit FLAC, 320 kbps MP3 with
  **joint stereo disabled** so the encoder never folds left and right (which
  would partly erase the binaural phase difference).

## 8. Things deliberately left out, and why

- **Solfeggio ladder as a tonal bed** (clashes with the Om drone; 1999 numerology).
- **"Lambda" 100–200 Hz and "epsilon" < 0.5 Hz beats** (Jeffrey Thompson's unvalidated terms); the 2.13 Hz floor already sits in low delta.
- **A continuous 40 Hz bed**: the clinical 40 Hz protocols do run for an hour, but here it would compete with the theta/delta floor, so gamma is given as cues.
- **Infrasound** (not reproducible on headphones, no evidence).
- **Monroe's "septon" multi-carrier stacking and the 2877 Hz tape tone** (the isochronic layers already supply the monaural component; a continuous high tone is unpleasant).
- **Hyperventilation or breath-hold blocks (Wim Hof, "pineal breath")** in a lying-down hypnagogic session: unsafe; the guide puts any such practice seated and before the session.
- **Any sampled ceremonial recording** (icaros, joik, Yolngu manikay, Gyuto chant): all voices are synthesised.

## 9. What the evidence supports, plainly

What a first listen can honestly deliver: deep relaxation and reduced state
anxiety (the most replicated binaural effect; Padmanabhan 2005), slowed sense of
time, heaviness and "body asleep" at 10–20 minutes, hypnagogic imagery and
"vibration" sensations in the theta and delta sections (standard hypnagogia,
intensified by expectation and the journey instructions), and an alert return.

What no sound can honestly promise: changing the pineal gland (it responds to
light, not sound; calcification is age-related and no acoustic study has
touched it), releasing DMT, guaranteed visions, manifestation, or travel between
dimensions or times. Those words appear in this project as the traditions'
own frames (Monroe's Focus 15 "no time", the Bardo, the Ogdoad), which is how
they are labelled throughout.
