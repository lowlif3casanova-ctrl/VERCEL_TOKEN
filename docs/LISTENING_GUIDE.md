# Pineal Gateway — listening guide

Three renders: **the full journey (48 minutes)**, **the daily session (15
minutes)** and **the sleep variant (40 minutes: the full arc to the floor,
then it stays there and fades out, with no call-back, no 40 Hz cues and no
re-alerting ramp)**. All are binaural: **stereo headphones are required** (over-ear or
in-ear; not bone-conduction, not speakers, not one earbud). The tones in the
left and right ears differ by a few hertz, and the "beat" the track is built
around exists only when each ear hears its own tone.

The minute-by-minute breathing protocol, the technique library, the physics
sheet, the goal map and the playback settings are in
`Pineal_Gateway_Breath_Protocol.xlsx`. This guide is the journey script.

## iPhone, AirPods and other spatial-audio players

No audio file can switch a phone's spatialiser off by itself, so set these
once (full table with menu paths in the workbook's *Playback Setup* sheet):

- **Spatialize Stereo / Spatial Audio: Off** (not Fixed, not Head Tracked).
  Control Center → press and hold the volume slider → Spatialize Stereo → Off;
  or Settings → Bluetooth → ⓘ next to your AirPods → Spatial Audio → Off.
- **Personalized Spatial Audio: off.** Same ⓘ page.
- **Mono Audio: off** and **Balance: centred.** Settings → Accessibility →
  Audio & Visual. Mono Audio sums the two ears and removes the binaural beat.
- **Conversation Awareness: off** (AirPods Pro 2 / AirPods 4). It ducks the
  track every time you hum or tone.
- **Headphone Accommodations: off; EQ off; no "Late Night".**
- Noise Cancellation on (or off), not Adaptive or Transparency.

The session is built to degrade gracefully if any of this is missed: the
pulsed layers, the bowls and the drum carry the beat acoustically, and the
verification report shows the entrainment rate survives a simulated
spatialiser within 0.03 Hz (as a monaural rather than purely binaural beat).
Android, Windows and macOS equivalents are in the workbook.

## Before you press play

**Volume.** Clearly audible but soft, quieter than conversation, about 40–60 %
of the device's range. Research sessions ran at roughly 65–70 dB at the ear;
the file cannot set that for you, so set it by ear once and leave it. If you
find yourself turning it up to "feel more", turn it back down: the beat is a
subtle percept and louder does not make it clearer, it only loads your ears.

**Room and body.** Darkness or an eye mask (the pineal gland responds to light,
not sound, so darkness is the one honest "pineal" lever you have), a stable
warm temperature with a light blanket (core temperature drops in deep
relaxation), a light stomach, empty bladder, phone silenced. Lie on your back
with the head slightly raised (it keeps you from dropping straight into
sleep), or sit upright if you tend to fall asleep. Water and a notebook within
reach.

**Time of day.** Evening or early morning in dim or red light; no bright
screens for two to three hours before an evening session. For the dream- and
vision-oriented use, the strongest window is wake-back-to-bed: sleep at least
five hours, stay up 30–60 minutes in dim light, then lie down with the track.
It costs sleep and makes sleep-paralysis episodes more likely, so not every
night.

**How often.** The full session once a day, or the daily session up to twice.
The two small longitudinal studies that exist used daily sessions for 60 days
(8 people) or two to four sessions a week for four weeks (15 people); give it
that long before judging what it does for you, and keep the weekly log the
workbook describes.

**Who should sit this out.** Epilepsy or a seizure history, a history of
psychosis, mania, dissociative disorder or PTSD with active flashbacks (deep
trance without a guide can destabilise), severe tinnitus or hyperacusis, and
anyone under 18. If you take psychiatric medication, check with your
prescriber first. Do not use it with psychedelics, cannabis or alcohol. Never
while driving, cycling, operating machinery or in water: the theta and delta
sections measurably lower vigilance.

## The preparation (4 minutes, before the track or during the Threshold)

This is the Gateway preparatory sequence from Robert Monroe's lineage combined
with the Yoga Nidra *sankalpa* and the shamanic statement of intent. Do it
every time: the research on drumming (Gingras 2014) found that journey-type
experiences came almost entirely from the instructions (10 of 10 people given
journey instructions, 2 of 8 without), not from the drum itself. Set and
setting carry a large part of what you will experience.

1. **The box (30 s).** Picture a heavy box. Put into it every worry, task and
   identity you do not need for the next hour. Close the lid. You get them
   back afterwards.
2. **Resonant tuning (2 min).** When the Om drone has fully come in (by 0:40),
   breathe in for four counts and hum on the out-breath, letting your voice
   find the drone or its octave. Feel the hum in the skull. Six to eight hums.
   This is Monroe's "resonant tuning" and the hatha-yoga *bhramari* (Hatha Yoga
   Pradipika 2.68); the measurable effects of chanting come from *your own*
   sound, not from listening.
3. **The statement (30 s).** Say, silently, once, in the present tense, what you
   are here for. Monroe's version opens: *"I am more than my physical body.
   Because I am more than physical matter, I can perceive that which is
   greater than the physical world."* (The full Gateway affirmation goes on to
   ask for guidance and protection; use it whole if you use it.) The Yoga Nidra
   version is a short positive resolve (*sankalpa*), stated three times, one
   historical ancestor of what is now called manifestation (the modern
   vocabulary comes from New Thought: the Kybalion, 1908, and Neville Goddard).
   The shamanic version is a single clear question or destination.
4. **The egg (30 s).** Feel light gather at the crown, flow out and down around
   the outside of the body to the feet, and come back up to the crown, leaving
   you inside a closed, protected shell (Monroe's "resonant energy balloon").
   Then let the track carry you.

## The journey, stage by stage (full session)

Every stage boundary is marked by a temple bell. The timings are here so you
know what is happening and what to do; the breath for each stage is in the
workbook's *Protocol 48 min* sheet.

### 1. Threshold (0:00–4:00) — tinkling, bell, Om
Alpha beat at 10 Hz. A pair of tingsha, a temple bell, the Om drone, then the
first bowls on the fifth and the fourth of Om, a little to the right (the
Hatha Yoga Pradipika says to listen for the inner sound in the right ear). A soft ocean-like swell paces the breath:
eight breaths a minute at first, easing to six by minute 3. **Do:** the
preparation above. Breathe with the swell.

### 2. Descent (4:00–11:00) — the body goes to sleep
The beat glides from 10 to 7.83 Hz (the Schumann fundamental) over five minutes
and holds. A low Gyuto-style chord drone enters, the breath pacer continues, a
bowl strikes at 6:00 and 8:30, a long horn swells at 9:18. **Do:** Ujjayi with
a long exhale and So'ham; let the body get heavy. This is the "mind awake, body
asleep" stage (Monroe's Focus 10). Minutes 6–11 are quiet on purpose: this is
the **manifestation / SATS window** (Neville Goddard's "state akin to sleep").
Hold one brief first-person scene that implies your intention is already true,
with sensory detail, and loop it gently. The bowl at 8:30 opens a 68-second
span if you like the Abraham-Hicks "pure thought" count; it is a harmless
marker. From 9:00, kriya-style spinal breathing if you practise it.

### 3. Drum Gate (11:00–21:00) — the journey
Distant thunder opens the gate. A frame drum begins at 4.25 Hz (255 beats a
minute: the tempo Gingras et al. 2014 measured on Harner's own recording; the
Foundation for Shamanic Studies itself says "about five beats a second") and
plays for nine minutes, unaccented, with a theta pulse underneath, a
didgeridoo breathing at 68 Hz and a stone-chamber drone at 102 Hz. The
binaural beat settles at 4.25 Hz. **Do:** the core shamanic method. Picture a real place you know with an
opening into the earth (a spring, a cave, roots, a hole). Enter it when the
drum is steady, travel down, and let whatever you meet come to you. Do not
force images; attend to the drum when the mind wanders. Let the breath go
free. If nothing comes, rest in the sound; that is also the practice.

### 4. Ajna (21:00–29:00) — the seven vowels
The beat rises to 6 Hz. A choir sings the seven Greek vowels A E Ē I O U Ō, one
pure-ratio step higher each time, then I-A-Ō on the octave, while seven bowls
climb the chakra ladder. **Do:** bring attention to the point between the
eyebrows (*ajna*, "two-petalled, bright as the autumn moon"; the Taoist
"turning the light around"), eyes closed and rolled gently upward (Shambhavi
mudra). Tone each vowel with the choir on your exhale, as low and relaxed as
you can. Let the attention rise with the ladder: root, sacral, solar plexus,
heart, throat, brow, crown.

### 5. No-Time (29:00–37:00) — the vibrational state
The outer tones drop to 3 Hz and then to 2.13 Hz, the floor of the session,
while the Om tone keeps a 10 Hz alpha beat, a Monroe-style layering meant to
keep awareness present as the body goes very deep. Long horns swell, bowls ring
at the slow rate, a five-second surge rides on the drone (Monroe's description
of the vibrations), and three 90-second 40 Hz "clarity" pulses pass through
(the third spills over the bell into Crown). At the end three thunders pass by.
**Do:** nothing with the breath except rest in the two pauses, at the top of
the inhale and the bottom of the exhale. If you feel buzzing, surging,
pressure, floating or a rolling sensation, let it "smooth and speed up" rather
than pushing or fearing it. If you feel yourself sliding into sleep, count
breaths backwards from 27. If imagery or sound comes, watch it as you would a
dream. The Bardo instruction for the thunders: *this is the natural sound of
your own mind; recognise it; it cannot harm you.* If you fall asleep, you fall
asleep.

### 6. Crown (37:00–43:00) — the clear light
The beat climbs back to 7.83 Hz. Three voices chant A (forehead), OM (throat),
HUM (heart) in the Bön three-syllable form; the chord drone returns; conch
clusters call from either side; one last 90-second 40 Hz pulse passes at 41:00;
a whisper of high "air" opens. **Do:** come back into the body from the top
down, toning every other syllable on your exhale: white light at the forehead
on A, red at the throat on OM, blue at the heart on HUM.

### 7. Return (43:00–48:00) — the call-back
The drum returns with the call-back: four sevens, thirty seconds of fast
drumming, four sevens, then slow grounding strokes (the fast burst and the slow
grounding are the Foundation for Shamanic Studies pattern; the sevens are a
widespread workshop convention). The beat rises 7.83 → 10 → 14.1 Hz so that you
come back alert, not groggy. A single 963 Hz bowl rings once in the clear at
45:20. Bowls on the fifth and fourth of Om and the tingsha close the circle. **Do:** when you hear the fast
drumming, take three physiological sighs (two inhales, one long exhale),
retrace your path and come back by the way you went in. Breathe with the pacer,
stretch on the last bowls, open your eyes slowly, write down everything before
you speak to anyone.

## The daily session (15 minutes)

Same arc, compressed: Threshold (0–2), Descent to 6 Hz (2–5), Drum Gate at 4.25
Hz (5–10), No-Time at 3 Hz with the 10 Hz alpha layer and 40 Hz cues plus
A-OM-HUM (10–12.5), call-back and Return to 14.1 Hz with the 963 Hz bowl in the
clear (12.5–15). Its length echoes the shorter of the two Foundation for
Shamanic Studies drum recordings (15 and 30 minutes), though here the drum
itself runs about five minutes. Use it on days you cannot give 48.

## What to expect, honestly

On a first listen people commonly report some of the following, roughly in
this order: heaviness and warmth in the first 10–20 minutes; a slowed or lost
sense of time; a wandering, dreamlike attention with fragments of imagery in
the drum and Ajna stages; and, in No-Time, buzzing or surging in the body, a
sense of floating or of the body's outline dissolving, sounds that are not in
the track, brief gaps in continuity, occasionally a jolt or a flash of fear.
These are the normal features of the hypnagogic state (the border of sleep),
which the track is built to reach and hold; expectation and the instructions
above intensify them. None of this is required, many people notice little the
first few times, and many simply fall asleep, which is fine.

Over weeks of daily use the evidence is thin and preliminary: two small pilot
studies (8 and 15 people, no control group) reported lower anxiety and better
quality of life; single sessions reliably lower state anxiety for a while; a
change in attention or creative flow showed up in one study, for some people
only. Nothing has been measured for sleep.

What this track cannot do, and what no sound can honestly claim: open,
decalcify or "activate" the pineal gland (it responds to light; calcification
is age-related and no acoustic study has ever changed it), release DMT, give
you knowledge you did not have, make wishes come true, or move you between
dimensions or times. Those phrases appear in this project because they are
the frames of the traditions the design draws on (Monroe's "no time", the
Bardo, the seven heavens of the Hermetic texts), and they are labelled that
way in `DESIGN.md`. The linking of the brow centre to the pineal gland is
itself a modern idea (Theosophy, 1888 onward); the classical texts never
mention the gland. If "travel between realms by sound" is what you are after,
the living traditions closest to it are Sant Mat's Surat Shabd Yoga (following
the inner sound current) and the Sikh *anhad shabad*: both are practices of
listening inward, and the inner-sound techniques in the workbook are their
entry. The practice is real. The promises are not ours to make.

## If something goes wrong

- **Stop now** for chest pain, panic that does not ease within five minutes of
  sitting up with eyes open, or ringing or muffled hearing. Ringing that lasts
  more than an hour after a session means the level was too high.
- **Fear during No-Time or the thunders:** open your eyes, feel your feet, name
  five things in the room, breathe out slowly. Skip ahead to the Return if you
  want. It passes.
- **Sleep-paralysis-like episode (cannot move, presence in the room):** the
  body-asleep state outlasting the mind. Move the eyes, wiggle a finger or
  toe, breathe; it passes by itself within seconds to a few minutes.
- **Pause for a week or more** if you notice a sense of unreality lasting hours,
  intrusive memories, insomnia or sleep paralysis most nights, mood swings, or
  feeling special or "chosen". The one controlled 30-minute theta study found
  confusion and fatigue items rose, on average, straight after a single
  session; treat them as a reason to pause. If any of this persists beyond a
  week or two, talk to a clinician. The track is a meditation aid, not a
  treatment.
